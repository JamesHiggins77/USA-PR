# The American Republic: history

How this America got here. The story diverges from ours with the deadlocked election of 1912; real people appear, but their careers differ. Generated from `js/data/lore.js`, which the game's History tab also reads.

## The constitution

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1789–1912 | The same republic as ours: a President elected every four years, a House elected by districts, and two dominant parties, the Democrats and the Republicans. |
| 1912 | Theodore Roosevelt’s breakaway Progressive Party beats the Republicans for second place. Woodrow Wilson wins the presidency with 42% of the vote, and no party controls the House. A year of deadlock follows: three appropriations bills fail, and the Army goes unpaid for six weeks. |
| 1913–1914 | The Reconstruction Amendments of 1914. A Prime Minister takes over the day-to-day running of the government. The President appoints and may dismiss the Prime Minister, who also needs the confidence of the House. The House is elected by statewide proportional representation. The President serves a six-year term, elected in two rounds: a national popular vote, then an Electoral College runoff between the top two. Three senators per state are elected by proportional representation and the fourth by the state house. Presidents may serve two terms. Wilson resigns to seek a fresh mandate under the new rules, and loses the runoff to Theodore Roosevelt. |
| 1974 | After a decade in which a dozen parties sat in the House, the House is fixed at 600 seats: 500 state list seats with no threshold, and 100 national list seats for parties with at least 5% of the nationwide vote. |
| 1990s | Regional parties are on the rise and the far left is fragmented. The presidency remains the one office every voter fills on the same day. |

## The parties

### Liberal Democratic Party (LDP)

*Founded 1966.* Born when the old Democratic Party broke apart over the Civil Rights and Voting Rights Acts, which Prime Minister Lyndon Johnson forced through against President Nixon’s wishes after John F. Kennedy’s assassination. Johnson and Hubert Humphrey led the northern, western and Black Democratic wings out of a party whose southern caucus had fought the laws to the end. The conservative southern Democrats who stayed behind later merged into the Conservative Coalition.

**Ideology:** Liberal and socially progressive; pro-market but committed to a welfare state; internationalist. A broad church running from Third Way New Democrats to civil-rights liberals, held together by opposition to segregation and later by government office.

### Social Democratic Labor Party (SDLP)

*Founded 1952.* A merger of the heirs of Theodore Roosevelt’s Progressive Party with the labor and socialist parties that had grown up beside it: the Socialist Party of Norman Thomas, Minnesota’s Farmer-Labor Party, New York’s American Labor Party and the Nonpartisan League of the Plains. Its strength lies in the industrial Midwest, the unions and the Rust Belt. Its high point came in 1968, when Robert F. Kennedy, beaten for the LDP nomination by Lyndon Johnson, joined the party and won the presidency, which he held for two terms.

**Ideology:** Mainstream social democracy and labor politics: collective bargaining, public health insurance, industrial policy and a mixed economy. It is wary of free trade and more culturally moderate than the LDP.

### Christian Democratic Party (CDP)

*Founded 1946.* Founded after the Second World War by Midwestern moderate Republicans, Catholic Democrats uneasy with the New Deal’s urban machines, and Protestant reformers, on the model of the Christian democratic parties then rebuilding Europe. It served as the balancing party of the 1950s.

**Ideology:** Pragmatic, centre-right Christian democracy: market economics with a social conscience, family policy, subsidiarity and federalism, Atlanticism. Culturally Christian but not sectarian.

### Conservative Coalition (CC)

*Founded 1978.* A merger of the rump southern Democratic Party, left behind by the 1966 split, with the Republican Party, after a decade in which the two had voted together in the House and taken turns blocking each other in the South. Ronald Reagan’s 1980 presidential win made it the main party of the right for a generation.

**Ideology:** Conservative: lower taxes, deregulation, a strong military and traditional values, with a southern populist streak and a business wing that do not always agree.

### American Solidarity Party (ASP)

*Founded 1988.* A confederation of centrist, faith-based regional parties that kept finding themselves on the same side of budget votes: the Mormon Unity Party in Utah and Idaho, the Catholic Party in the Great Lakes cities, and the Moderate Party in New England. After the 1974 reform they formed a joint list to reach the 5% needed for national list seats.

**Ideology:** Christian democratic in the older sense: culturally conservative and fiscally progressive. Pro-family, pro-labor and devolutionist, it bargains with left or right for its constituent states.

### Reform Party (REF)

*Founded 1995.* Founded by Ross Perot after his 1992 presidential campaign won 19% of the first-round vote. It drew in independents, deficit hawks and opponents of free trade, and agreed confidence and supply for Gore’s government in 1996. Its best days may already be behind it.

**Ideology:** Populist centrism: balanced budgets, term limits, campaign finance reform, opposition to NAFTA, and a distrust of both big parties.

### United Left (UL)

*Founded (not yet founded).* A United Left alliance has been proposed many times since the 1970s and has never got off the ground.

**Ideology:** An electoral alliance of the far left.

### Green Party (GRN)

*Founded 1984.* Grew out of the anti-nuclear and environmental movements of the 1970s and early 1980s, alongside the state Green parties that later formed the American Green Alliance. A marginal force until Ralph Nader took the leadership in the late 1990s.

**Ideology:** Eco-socialist and progressive: environmental protection, grassroots democracy, social justice and non-violence, with deep hostility to corporate power.

### Communist Party USA (CPUSA)

*Founded 1919.* The Communist Party USA, founded in the wake of the Russian Revolution. It peaked in the 1930s labor struggles, was hounded in the 1950s, and lost most of its members after 1956. It held one or two House seats under PR in most decades.

**Ideology:** Marxist-Leninist, though by the 1990s mainly a party of labor militancy and civil rights.

### Democratic Socialists of America (DSA)

*Founded 1982.* Democratic Socialists of America, formed by left-wing SDLP dissidents and New Left veterans who thought the SDLP had made its peace with capitalism.

**Ideology:** Democratic socialism: social ownership, union power, universal public services, achieved through elections.

### Working Families Party (WFP)

*Founded 1994.* The Working Families Party, built by New York unions and community organizations as a pressure party that endorses candidates of other parties as often as it runs its own.

**Ideology:** Labor-progressive: living wages, paid leave, affordable housing.

### Revolutionary socialist parties (RSP)

*Founded various.* The fragmented revolutionary left: the Socialist Workers, Workers World, the Party for Socialism and Liberation, the Socialist Equality Party, the Black Panther Party and others, each with its own newspaper and its own split.

**Ideology:** Revolutionary socialism and communism in rival Trotskyist, Maoist and Black liberation traditions.

### American Communist Party (ACP)

*Founded (2020s).* Does not exist in this period.

**Ideology:** Syncretic far left.

### Libertarian Party (LIB)

*Founded 1971.* Founded in Colorado by activists angry at price controls and the Vietnam draft. It benefits from PR, which gives it seats in most parliaments.

**Ideology:** Classical liberal: low taxes, minimal government, civil liberties, a non-interventionist foreign policy.

### Constitution Party (CON)

*Founded 1980.* Founded as the American Constitutional Party by conservatives who refused to follow the Republicans into the 1978 merger with the southern Democrats, which they saw as an unprincipled power grab. It is the main protest party of the hard right.

**Ideology:** Paleoconservative and Christian-right: strict constitutionalism, states’ rights, opposition to abortion and immigration.

### MAGA Party (MAGA)

*Founded (2016 in the default timeline).* Does not exist in this period.

**Ideology:** National populism.

### America First Party (AFP)

*Founded (later).* Does not exist in this period.

**Ideology:** Hard-right nationalism.

### American Green Alliance (AGA)

*Founded 1991.* A federation of state Green parties that would not join a national party run from Washington, ranging from progressive Rhode Island Greens to Virginia’s conservationist Independent Greens.

**Ideology:** Environmentalism without a fixed left-right position; every chapter sets its own line.

### Lone Star Party (LSP)

*Founded 1986.* Born in the Texas oil bust of the mid-1980s, when Texans blamed federal energy policy and Washington’s banks for the state’s ruin. Ron Paul brought it national attention.

**Ideology:** Texan regionalism with a libertarian-populist streak: state control of resources and taxes, gun rights, low federal taxes. A hardline minority wants independence.

### Southern Rights Party (SRP)

*Founded 1948.* The States’ Rights Democratic Party, the Dixiecrats, which walked out of the Democratic convention over Truman’s civil-rights platform and nominated Strom Thurmond for President. Unlike in our history it never went back, and George Wallace made it a force in 1968.

**Ideology:** Southern regionalism: social conservatism, economic populism, states’ rights. Its hardliners still talk of Southern independence.

### Californian National Party (CNP)

*Founded 1992.* Founded after the Los Angeles riots and the recession of the early 1990s by Californians who felt the state paid Washington far more than it got back.

**Ideology:** Centre-left Californian nationalism, seeking independence in the long run.

### Californian Freedom Coalition (CFC)

*Founded 1978.* Grew out of the Proposition 13 tax revolt. Its founders wanted Sacramento, not Washington, to set taxes, water and land policy.

**Ideology:** Centre-right devolutionism: more powers for California within the Union.

### Alaskan Party (ALK)

*Founded 1984.* Founded by Joe Vogler as the Alaskan Independence Party. Walter Hickel won the governorship on its ticket in 1990.

**Ideology:** Alaskan nationalism, libertarian and conservative; it calls for a referendum on Alaska’s status.

### Aloha ʻĀina Party (AʻĀ)

*Founded 1993.* Founded after the centenary of the overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom and Congress’s apology for it.

**Ideology:** Native Hawaiian sovereignty and self-determination, land rights, environmental protection.

### Raza Unida Party (RUP)

*Founded 1970.* La Raza Unida Party, founded in Crystal City, Texas, during the Chicano movement. PR kept it alive after the movement faded.

**Ideology:** Mexican-American civil rights, community control, labor rights.

### New England National Party (NENP)

*Founded 1990.* A New England regionalist party founded during the region’s banking crisis by Yankee independents and Vermont separatists.

**Ideology:** Regional autonomy for New England, town-meeting democracy, decentralism.

### New York Freedom Party (NYFP)

*Founded 1975.* Founded during New York City’s fiscal crisis by New Yorkers who believed the state sent Washington and the rest of the country far more than it got back.

**Ideology:** New York regionalism: fiscal autonomy and a better deal for the state within the Union.

### Vermont Progressive Party (VPP)

*Founded 1981.* Grew from the coalition that elected Bernie Sanders mayor of Burlington. The strongest third force in Vermont politics.

**Ideology:** Progressive and populist, with deep local roots.

## The default timeline

In the default timeline, Donald Trump founds the MAGA Party and wins the presidency in 2016, and Mike Pence (CC) leads a broad-right coalition of the CC, MAGA, CDP, ASP and Libertarians. In this game, none of that is guaranteed.
